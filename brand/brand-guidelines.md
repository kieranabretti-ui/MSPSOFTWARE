# Headroom brand guidelines

Version 1.0 — 6 October 2026. Written for the designer or developer who joins next month.

Everything here describes what is built and shipping in this repository. Where something is a
convention for work not yet done, it says so. The tokens are the contract: if a rule here and
`brand/brand-tokens.css` disagree, the CSS wins and this document is wrong.

Every figure quoted in these pages (£4,281, £356 a month, £4,272, £143,220, 40 opportunities, 15 clients)
is **demo data**: the analysis of Northlight IT, a fictional MSP generated by `src/demo/`. There are
no customers yet, and no customer figure, logo, testimonial, benchmark or integration appears
anywhere in this brand.

## 1. The brand in one page

**Name.** Headroom.
**Tagline.** Find the work your MSP is doing for free.
**What it is.** A financial and commercial intelligence product for managed service providers. It
reads the exports an MSP already has and finds revenue leakage, with the evidence behind every pound.
**Who it is for.** Owners and commercial leads of MSPs with roughly 5 to 50 staff. Commercially
sharp, often ex-technicians, short on time, sceptical of vendors.
**The feeling.** A serious, premium financial product. Calm about problems, confident about fixes.
**The look.** An ink-dark foundation, bone type, one electric lime accent that only ever means money
found or recovered. Host Grotesk throughout, tabular figures on every number that can change.
**The signature.** The GapBar: what you billed in neutral, the unbilled gap in lime, a 2px gap
between them.
**The hard rule.** Figures are *potential* leakage to review. Never promised, never guaranteed.

## 2. Positioning

Headroom is a financial and commercial intelligence product for MSPs, not an IT tool. It talks about
money, margin and revenue, not about tickets and endpoints as technology. The mechanism is a
deterministic rules engine (`src/engine/`) that ties every pound back to the ticket, time entry,
device, charge or contract clause behind it.

**What we are**

- The commercial audit an MSP has never had time to run.
- Evidence first: an opportunity without its ticket, clause or timesheet is not an opportunity.
- Something an owner takes into a client review and acts on the same week.

**What we are not**

- Not a cybersecurity or data-leak product. ("Leak" read as a breach to this audience; it is one of
  the reasons the working name MSP Leak was dropped.)
- Not accounting software, not a PSA, not generic MSP software.
- Not an AI chatbot. The engine is deterministic; AI explains one opportunity on request and never
  invents evidence.
- Not childish, gimmicky, hacker-themed or futuristic.

**The product principles** (from `PRODUCT.md`, and they govern design as much as code)

1. Every pound is traceable to evidence.
2. Money first, mechanics second.
3. Potential, never promised.
4. Calm about problems, confident about fixes.
5. Works with what the MSP already exports.

## 3. Personality

Confident. Intelligent. Commercial. Direct. Calm. Trustworthy.

Read it as a person: a sharp commercial director who has already done the work, shows you the
figure, shows you the evidence, tells you what to do, and does not oversell it.

| Trait | What it means in the product |
|---|---|
| Confident | States the figure plainly. No hedging adverbs, no exclamation marks. |
| Intelligent | Shows its working. Every opportunity carries its evidence, its calculation and a confidence level with its basis. |
| Commercial | Talks in pounds, margin and MRR, not in features. |
| Direct | Short sentences. The action is named: bill it, correct the agreement, reprice. |
| Calm | Problems are reported in neutral type, not in red panels. Danger is rationed. |
| Trustworthy | "Potential" everywhere. Demo data is labelled as demo data on every surface. |

## 4. Brand architecture

**Headroom is the company and the platform**, not a feature. Leakage detection is the first product.
The name was chosen partly because it expands: it scored 8.7 of 10 on expandability, the highest of
the 48 names in the exercise.

The naming pattern for future products is **Headroom + the commercial noun**, for example
*Headroom Leakage*, *Headroom Pricing*, *Headroom Forecast*. This is a pattern, not a roadmap: none of
those products exist and none is committed.

Rules for the pattern:

- **Do** keep "Headroom" first and unmodified, in the wordmark's own setting.
- **Do** use a plain commercial noun that an MSP owner already uses.
- **Don't** invent sub-brands with their own marks, colours or type. One mark, one palette.
- **Don't** say or write "Headroom Leak", "HeadroomAI", "HR" or "Headroom by …".

In running copy the product is "Headroom", never "the Headroom platform". The first mention in
marketing copy may be "Headroom for MSPs" where search needs the modifier, because headroom is a
common word.

## 5. Taglines

Ten lines in the brand voice. All are owner-voiced, specific and free of promises.

| # | Tagline | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Find the work your MSP is doing for free.** | The chosen line. |
| 2 | Your contracts say one thing. Your tickets say another. | Names the mechanism in the owner's own words. |
| 3 | Bill what you already deliver. | Shortest; an instruction, not a claim. |
| 4 | Every pound, traced to the ticket behind it. | Leads on evidence rather than loss. |
| 5 | The margin you already earned. | Calm, premium; vaguer about what the product does. |
| 6 | Know what every client actually costs you. | Profitability-first; points at the roadmap, not today. |
| 7 | The commercial audit your MSP has never run. | Good for a subject line; long for a lockup. |
| 8 | Agreements are signed once. Clients change every month. | A statement of the problem; needs a second line to sell. |
| 9 | See the revenue your agreements stopped covering. | Accurate, but "stopped covering" needs a beat to land. |
| 10 | Nobody has time to check a thousand tickets against fifteen contracts. | True and specific; a body line, not a tagline. |

**Top three**

1. **Find the work your MSP is doing for free.**
2. **Your contracts say one thing. Your tickets say another.**
3. **Bill what you already deliver.**

**Why the first one wins.** It is the only one that is simultaneously specific ("the work"),
owner-voiced (it is how an MSP owner describes the problem to another owner), and uncomfortable
without being rude: "for free" is the sting, and the owner supplies the anger themselves. It names
labour rather than data, which keeps the product away from the cybersecurity reading that sank the
working name. It is an instruction to the product as much as a claim — the product literally finds
it — so it never overpromises. And it pairs with a calm, abstract name: *Headroom* carries the
company, the tagline carries today's product. If the product line widens into pricing and
forecasting, the tagline can change; the name does not have to.

Use the tagline in full, with the full stop, sentence case. It lives in `src/brand/brand.ts` as
`BRAND.tagline` — never retype it. It appears on the auth pages, in the landing footer and in the
page title.

## 6. Voice and copy

British English. Second person ("your clients"). Sentence case everywhere except the small uppercase
column labels. The Oxford comma is not used.

### Rules

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "£4,281 potential leakage identified" | "We found you £4,281!" |
| "Recover £356 a month" | "Unlock hidden revenue" |
| "Work the agreement excludes, done for free" | "Scope creep issues detected" |
| "Review findings" | "Explore your insights" |
| "Figures are estimates of potential revenue" | "Guaranteed recovery" |
| "Potential leakage" | "Lost revenue" as a settled fact |
| "£72 a month" | "£72/month" in prose (the slash is fine inside a dense table cell: `£72/mo`) |
| "15 clients", "40 opportunities" | "fifteen clients" in UI; spell out only in marketing prose |
| "Demo data: Northlight IT … are fictional" | Any customer, logo, testimonial or benchmark |

### Numbers

- Money is `Intl.NumberFormat('en-GB', GBP)` with no decimals in the UI (`money()` in
  `src/lib/format.ts`). Decimals only where pence genuinely matter.
- Every number that can change is tabular (`tnum`). See [Typography](#9-typography).
- Percentages: whole numbers, except the leakage share, which takes one decimal ("2.9%").
- Hours: `1.5h`, `1h 20m`.
- Ranges and periods come from the data: "April to September 2026".

### The words we use

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| opportunity (each item the analysis raises) | finding, issue, alert, insight (`Finding` stays as the code type) |
| potential leakage | lost revenue, losses |
| recover | reclaim, win back |
| agreement, contract | MSA, deal |
| client | customer, account (an MSP's customers are clients) |
| evidence | proof, receipts |
| out-of-scope work, unbilled work, agreement drift, underpriced clients | leakage types. Marketing may head out-of-scope work "Scope creep" once, tied to the label "Out-of-scope work" (the landing page's problem ledger does) |
| confidence level: High, Medium, Low, each with its basis | a confidence score or percentage (the percentage survives only as `confidence_score` in the CSV) |
| recurring (£ a month), one-off (£ in the period) | MRR opportunity, monthly leakage for a one-off figure |
| analysis, run the analysis | scan, audit (in-app; "audit" is allowed in marketing) |

Category names are fixed in `src/lib/labels.ts`. Use them verbatim; do not paraphrase a category in
a new screen.

### State copy

Every state is written, not generic. Patterns that already ship:

- **Empty, nothing wrong:** name the state, then what would fill it. "Nothing open — Every finding in
  this analysis has been resolved or dismissed. Run a new analysis when fresh exports arrive."
- **Empty, something to do:** quantify the opportunity and give both routes ("Upload your data" /
  "Load demo data").
- **Loading:** say what is happening, not "Loading…", wherever the wait is longer than a moment. The
  demo run names its four stages.
- **Error:** what failed, in one line, and the way out. Errors never blame the user.
- **Blocked:** "Downloads are turned off in this hosted preview. Run Headroom locally to download
  files."
- **Disclaimer:** one sentence, shared, from `<Disclaimer />`. Never reworded per screen.

### Demo labelling

Northlight IT is a fictional demo MSP. Anywhere its figures appear — app banner, report cover, PDF,
landing page, auth pages — they carry a "Demo data" badge or sentence. Outside the app (decks,
emails, social), label the figure as demo data in the same visual field as the figure, not in a
footnote.

## 7. Visual direction

**A dark foundation with one electric accent.** The canvas is ink (#0a0b0d). Surfaces step upward in
very small lightness increments; separation comes from those steps and from hairline borders, not
from shadows. Type is bone (#edeef0) and its three quieter greys. One lime (#c4f25b) carries the
whole emotional load of the product: money found.

**Why dark.** The product is read at a desk, often for half an hour at a time, and it is mostly
figures. A dark field makes the figures the brightest thing on screen and makes a single accent
legible without shouting.

**The lime rule.** Lime means *money found or recovered*, and the one action per screen that leads to
money. Everything else is neutral. Lime is not a brand decoration: a lime border on a card that is
not about money is a bug.

**Quality references, not styles to copy:** Stripe, Linear, Ramp, Vercel, Mercury, Brex. What we take
from them is the standard — hairlines, restraint, density handled well, type doing the work — not
their gradients, their illustrations or their layouts. Do not reach for a reference's signature move
(Stripe's gradient mesh, Linear's radial glow) as a shortcut.

**Refusals.** The direction contracts for both surfaces state what this product refuses:

- No grey admin panel with a coloured pill on every row.
- No centred headline over a gradient followed by three feature cards.
- No grid of same-size icon cards used as page structure.
- No shadows used as decoration. Depth is a surface step plus a hairline.
- No stock photography, no 3D renders, no illustration of abstract concepts.
- No product screenshots that are not the real product with real demo figures.

### 8.3 Semantic

Each role has a hue, a `-soft` fill at 12% and a `-line` hairline at 30%.

| Role | Value | Contrast | Means |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--brand-success` | `#4cd18f` | 9.6:1 | Resolved, recovered, healthy. |
| `--brand-warning` | `#f2b84b` | 10.4:1 | Above contract, needs watching, a mapping that will skip rows. |
| `--brand-danger` | `#f2735f` | 6.6:1 | Critical findings, below-target margin, errors. |
| `--brand-info` | `#74a9f6` | 7.8:1 | In progress, planned, neutral notices. |

**Semantic colour is never the only signal.** Every use is paired with an icon, a label, or a shape.
Health is a dot *and* a word. Action status is a dot whose *shape* differs (open is a hollow ring,
in progress is filled). Confidence is a word *and* a count of dots. Severity, shown as Priority on the
opportunity page only, is a label *and* a count of bars.

**Danger must not dominate.** The rules, as built:

- Only `CRITICAL` takes a coloured pill. `HIGH` colours its three severity bars amber and leaves the
  pill neutral, so a column of High findings stays calm. `MEDIUM` and `LOW` take no colour at all.
- Status tags such as "Open" are outlined and quiet, because nearly every row says Open.
- A below-target margin turns the figure red with an alert icon; it is never a red-filled cell. A
  margin just above target is deliberately left uncoloured, so amber only ever means "above contract".
- Danger buttons are a soft fill with a hairline, not a solid red block.

### 8.4 Where lime is allowed

This is the full inventory. If a new use is not on this list, it needs a decision, not a guess.

| Allowed | Example in the build |
|---|---|

| A positive trend on money found | `TrendIndicator` when the movement is good |
| The matched phrase in evidence | `<mark>` with `accent-soft` fill and `accent-line` underline |
| System accents: focus ring, caret, text selection, checkbox and radio | `src/index.css` |
| The active navigation item's icon | `AppLayout` sidebar |
| Progress and liveness: spinner, success tick | `Spinner`, toast |
| A chart's hover state | `TrendChart` `activeBar` |

## 9. Typography

**One family: Host Grotesk.** Self-hosted as a variable font
(`@fontsource-variable/host-grotesk`, weight axis 300–800), imported in `src/index.css`. The PDF
embeds the static 400 and 600 faces from `src/assets/fonts/`.

Instrument Sans was considered and rejected as an over-used default; Host Grotesk reads as a
contemporary grotesk without being the same grotesk as every other SaaS product, and its digits are
even-width, which the PDF depends on.

Two weights are in use: **400** for body and **600** for everything emphatic. Do not introduce 500
display type, 700 headings or a second family. `--font-mono` exists for data only (column names,
rule ids); it is the system mono stack, not a bought face.

**Display at marketing scale.** The landing page needs type that scales with the viewport, so
`src/pages/landing/primitives.tsx` sets the hero with `clamp(2.75rem, 1.15rem + 5.4vw, 5rem)` and
section titles with `clamp(1.875rem, 1.15rem + 2.4vw, 3rem)`, both sitting on the display tracking
token. These are not tokenised yet — see [Known drift](#21-known-drift-and-open-gaps).

**The headline figure in the app** uses `clamp(3rem, 7vw, var(--type-data-xl))` via
`<Figure size="xl">`, so £4,281 never overflows on a phone.

### 9.2 Figures

**Every number that can change is tabular.** Use the `tnum` utility
(`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`) or the `<Figure>` component, which applies it. This includes
money, counts, percentages, hours, confidence and dates in tables. A column of figures that jitters
as it updates is a defect.

```
<Figure size="xl" testId="hero-total">£4,281</Figure>   the money, bone
<Figure tone="accent">£356</Figure>                     money recovered or recurring, lime
<Figure size="md" tone="muted">£1,240</Figure>          a supporting figure
```

Figures are bone by default. They take the accent only when they are money found, recurring or
recovered.

### 9.3 Rules

- **Do** set headings in sentence case. "Clients at risk", not "Clients At Risk".
- **Do** keep the uppercase `text-label` for small column labels and nothing else. It is the only
  uppercase in the system.
- **Don't** put an uppercase eyebrow above a page title. (This was removed from Onboarding and the
  report cover deliberately.)
- **Do** cap measure: body at ~52–56 characters (`max-w-[52ch]`), marketing lead at ~46–48.
- **Do** use `text-balance` on short display headings.
- **Don't** use italics. Host Grotesk has an italic; the brand does not.
- **Don't** letterspace positive except `text-label`.

## 10. Logo system

### 10.1 The idea

**The lifted corner.** A solid square whose top-right quarter has been cut out and lifted clear, in
lime. The missing piece — the money that was always yours — found, and raised into the headroom
above the block. It reads as a quarter lifted out of a whole, which is exactly what the product does
to a client's revenue.

### 10.2 How it was chosen

Twelve directions were drawn across four concepts and scored by three independent reviewers on
distinctiveness, concept fit, small-size legibility, credibility, simplicity and lockup quality.

| Concept | Idea | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Level | A measured line with space above it | Legible but generic; read as a chart axis. |
| Monogram | An H built from the gap | Read as a letterform first, an idea second. |
| **Gap** | A square with a quarter lifted out | **Chosen: "lifted corner", 7.7 of 10, the highest score.** |
| Recovery | An arc returning a piece to its place | Too much motion to survive at 16px. |

The winner was then refined so that the lifted piece is *exactly* the quarter it came from, the notch
is cut one 2-unit channel wider than the piece, and the piece is lifted 4 units.

**Banned forever**, because every MSP and fintech already uses them: shields, padlocks, currency
symbols, magnifying glasses, lightning bolts, robots, leaking buckets, taps, droplets, upward arrows
made of money.

### 10.3 Construction

Drawn on a 32-unit grid. The paths are in `src/brand/Logo.tsx` as `MARK_BODY` and `MARK_PIECE`, and
the same paths appear in `brand/logo/*.svg` and `brand/favicons/favicon.svg`. The PDF redraws the
same geometry as vectors in `src/lib/pdfBrand.ts`.

| Element | Geometry |
|---|---|
| Body | A 20 × 20 square spanning x 6–26, y 8–28. Outer corners r3; the two inner corners of the notch r1. |
| Notch | 12 × 12, spanning x 14–26, y 8–20: the quarter (10) plus one 2-unit channel on each cut side. |
| Piece | A 10 × 10 square spanning x 16–26, y 4–14 — exactly the quarter, lifted 4 units. Top-right corner r3, the rest r1. |
| Mark content box | viewBox `6 4 20 24` (20 wide × 24 tall, including the lift). |
| App-icon tile | viewBox `0 0 32 32`, ink tile, corner radius 7. |

Every straight edge lands on an even grid unit, so at the 16px favicon (half a pixel per unit) every
edge falls on a whole pixel and the mark stays crisp. Only the corner arcs cross half pixels.

**Don't redraw the mark.** Reference the SVG, the React component, or the two path strings. If you
are ever changing it, change `MARK_BODY`/`MARK_PIECE` and regenerate everything from them.

### 10.4 The lockup

The mark is **1em tall, sits on the wordmark's baseline, with a 0.3em gap**. At that size the body
stands level with the ascenders and the lime piece rises above them — the lift is visible at every
size without the lockup growing a top-heavy margin.

Two alternatives were drawn and rejected:

- **A — mark block at cap height, baseline-aligned.** The block matched the H so closely that the mark
  read as a letter, and the lockup looked like "⌐Headroom".
- **C — mark at 1.2em, centred on the wordmark's optical centre.** Stronger as a standalone pair, but
  it floated above the baseline in dense chrome and broke the 56px top bar.

In code: `<Logo />` from `src/brand/Logo.tsx`, which sets the wordmark at 15px / 600 /
−0.02em tracking for app and marketing chrome. Use the component; do not re-assemble the lockup.

### 10.6 Clear space, minimum sizes and colourways

- **Clear space:** the width of the mark (20 grid units), on all four sides of the lockup or the mark.
  Nothing enters it — no type, no rule, no other logo, no card edge.
- **Minimum sizes:** lockup **80px wide**; bare mark **16px**. Below 80px, drop the wordmark and use
  the mark.
- **Colourways:** bone + lime on ink (default); ink + `#5a8700` on white; one-colour bone; one-colour
  ink; `currentColor` monochrome. Nothing else.

### 10.7 Misuse

**Don't:**

- recolour either part — no lime body, no bone piece, no brand-of-the-month gradient;
- fill the piece with a different green because it "pops better";
- rotate, mirror, skew, outline, emboss, add a shadow or a glow;
- stretch the lockup, or change the 0.3em gap or the 1em mark height;
- re-space or re-set the wordmark in another face;
- place the lockup on a busy photograph, on lime, or on a mid-grey that kills both parts;
- put the mark inside a circle, a shield or a rounded box other than the ink app tile;
- use the mark as a bullet, a loading spinner or a decorative pattern;
- pair it with a second logo inside the clear space.

## 11. Wordmark

"Headroom", one word, Host Grotesk 600, tracking −0.02em, sentence case — a capital H and nothing
else. Eight letters with a strong rhythm of ascenders (H, d) and round letters (e, a, o, o, m), which
is why the mark can stay abstract: the word carries the recognition.

- **Do** write it as Headroom in all copy, including the first word of a sentence and in URLs' display
  text.
- **Don't** write HEADROOM, headroom (as the company), HeadRoom, Head Room or H.
- **Don't** set the wordmark in another family, add a strapline inside the lockup, or run the tagline
  on the same line as the wordmark. The tagline is separate type, at least one line below.
- The product name inside UI copy is plain text in the surrounding style — it does not take the
  wordmark's tracking, and it is never lime.

## 13. Data visualisation

Charts carry the data in neutrals. The accent marks money found or recovered — nothing else. Rules
and components live in `src/components/charts.tsx`.

### 13.1 Universal rules

1. **Neutrals carry the data.** `--viz-series` by default, `--viz-series-strong` for the one bar or
   row that deserves emphasis (usually the latest month).
2. **One axis.** Horizontal gridlines only, in `--viz-grid`; no vertical gridlines, no axis lines, no
   tick marks.
3. **Tabular figures everywhere**, including tooltips and axis labels.
4. **Every chart has a text alternative.** Each chart element carries `role="img"` and an
   `aria-label` that reads the series out ("By month: April £712, May £560, …").
5. **No chart junk.** No 3D, no drop shadows on bars, no gradients, no background fill, no legend
   where a direct label will do.
6. **Money axis labels abbreviate**: `£4k`, `£4.3k`; the tooltip gives the exact figure.
7. **Never animate on data change.** The entry animation is 500ms and happens once; updates are
   instant.

### 13.2 The forms, and when to use each

| Form | Built? | Use it for | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GapBar** (the signature) | Yes | Billed against potential leakage, at any scale | Billed in `--viz-series`, the gap in the accent, a **2px gap** between the two parts, 3px end radii. A gap below 1.5% is still drawn at 1.5% so it stays visible. Captions read "Billed £143,220" and "Potential leakage £4,281 · 2.9%" (not "Unbilled": the total includes pricing estimates). |
| **Bar (monthly totals)** | Yes — `TrendChart` | Any series over discrete months | Vertical bars, 30% category gap, 4px top radii, latest month a step brighter, hover lights the bar in the accent. |
| **Horizontal share bars** | Yes — `ShareBars` | Breakdowns by category or client | One neutral hue, sorted largest first, every row labelled with its figure. This is the **preferred form over a donut.** |
| **LeakBar** (GapBar at row scale) | Yes | Client rows on Overview and Clients | A lime fill on a hairline track, sized to the row's leakage against the largest row, so rows read apart at a glance. The figure beside it carries the value. (A per-row billed-vs-unbilled bar was tried first: at 1 to 5% every row was the same grey line with a lime speck.) |
| **ConfidenceLevel** | Yes — `ConfidenceLevel` | An opportunity's confidence | Three 6px dots and a word in neutral ink: High fills three, Medium two, Low one. "High" in lists, "High confidence" plus its one-line basis on an opportunity. Never coloured, never a percentage. It replaces the old confidence bar. |
| **Trend indicator** | Yes — `TrendIndicator` | Change against the previous period | Arrow plus a whole-number percentage; accent when the movement is good news, `ink-2` when it is not, `ink-3` and "Flat" below 0.5%. Direction is in the icon and in screen-reader text, never in colour alone. |
| **Line chart** | Not built | A continuous series with enough points that bars become stripes (roughly 12+) | 1.5px line in `--viz-series-strong`, no area fill, no point markers except the last, single accent only if the line *is* money recovered. |
| **Donut** | Not built, and discouraged | Only a true part-to-whole where the parts are few and the shares are coarse | If it is ever justified: maximum four segments plus "Other", a 70% inner radius, the total in the hole, every segment directly labelled with its figure, no legend, and segment colours from `--viz-cat-*` in order. Prefer `ShareBars`. |

### 13.3 Financial metrics and comparison

- **The headline figure** is bone at `data-xl`, with its label *above* it in `text-body` and the
  qualifier below it ("Across 40 opportunities at 15 clients, measured against 6 months of
  agreement revenue."). Money found is never presented without its period and its denominator.
- **Recurring versus one-off:** the monthly figure takes the accent, the annualised figure stays bone.
  Annualised figures always carry "if the recurring leakage is left uncorrected".
- **Comparison cards** state *supported / contracted*, *actual / agreement*, *margin / target* —
  always actual first, the benchmark second and quieter. Anything above the contracted number is
  amber with an up arrow and screen-reader text; anything below target margin is red with the alert
  icon. A value inside tolerance is left uncoloured on purpose.
- **Totals rows** are compulsory on any table of money: the visible rows must add up on screen, and a
  filtered table totals what it shows.
- **A figure never appears without its unit and period.** "£356" is wrong; "£356 a month" is right.

## 14. UI language: components and states

The kit is `src/components/ui.tsx` (controls, containers, badges, states) and
`src/components/charts.tsx` (data). `src/pages/app/data/kit.tsx` holds `Select`, `Callout`,
`Dropzone` and `Steps`, which are kit candidates.

### 14.1 Shape, depth and motion

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--radius-xs` | 4px | Badges, chips, severity pills. |
| `--radius-sm` | 6px | Small controls, tags, icon buttons. |
| `--radius-md` | 8px | Buttons, inputs, selects. |
| `--radius-lg` | 12px | Cards, tables. |
| `--radius-xl` | 16px | Modals, panels, the hero. |
| `--radius-2xl` | 24px | Reserved for marketing frames. Unused today. |
| `--shadow-sm` → `elevate-1` | `0 1px 2px rgb(0 0 0 / .5)` | Reserved. Unused today. |
| `--shadow-md` → `elevate-2` | inset highlight + `0 12px 32px -12px` | Tooltips. |
| `--shadow-lg` → `elevate-3` | inset highlight + `0 32px 80px -24px` + ring | Modals, drawers, toasts, the busy overlay. |
| `--shadow-glow` → `glow-accent` | accent ring + `0 8px 40px -8px` lime | Reserved for a single money moment. Unused today. |
| `--ease-out` | `cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1)` | Everything. |
| `--duration-fast` / `--duration-base` | 120ms / 200ms | Controls / panels. |

**Depth comes from the surface step plus a hairline. Cards never carry a shadow** — only things that
float do. `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` collapses every animation and transition to 0.01ms
globally; the landing page's audit run is skipped entirely in that mode, and its final figures are
always in the DOM.

### 14.2 Controls

**Buttons** — `<Button variant size>` / `<ButtonLink>`. Heights 32 / 36 / 44px (`sm` / `md` / `lg`),
8px radius, 500 weight, 150ms colour transition, `disabled` at 45% opacity, `loading` shows a spinner
and sets `aria-busy`.

| Variant | Look | When |
|---|---|---|
| `primary` | Bone on ink (`bg-ink text-canvas`) | The default action on a screen. |
| `accent` | Lime with ink text | **One per screen, and only when it leads to money.** |
| `secondary` | Raised with a hairline | Everything alongside. |
| `ghost` | Text only until hovered | Tertiary actions in a header. |
| `danger` | Soft danger fill with a hairline | Destructive confirmation. Never a solid red block. |

**Inputs** — `inputCls`: 36px tall, 8px radius, sunken fill, strong border, accent border plus a 3px
`accent-soft` ring on focus. `<Field>` wraps label, hint and error; an error replaces the hint and is
`text-danger` at caption size. `<Select>` stays a real `<select>` with a drawn chevron.

**Links** — `<TextLink>` is quiet until hovered, then underlines at a 4px offset. Body links keep a
3px underline offset and a 1px thickness globally.

### 14.3 Containers

- `<Card>` — 12px radius, hairline, surface fill. No shadow.
- `<CardHeader title subtitle right>` — h3 title, muted subtitle, divider below.
- `<PageHeader title subtitle actions>` — h1 and up to three actions, 28px of space below.
- Section headings inside a page are h2 (20px) with a 13px subtitle, or h3 when quiet.
- `<Modal>` — 16px radius, `elevate-3`, blurred overlay, Escape closes, footer actions right-aligned.
  On a phone it becomes a bottom sheet.

### 14.4 Status, confidence, priority and health

- `<ConfidenceLevel level short?>` — three small dots plus the word, in neutral ink, with the level's
  definition in its title. Lists, the overview and the landing page show it where severity used to sit.
- `<SeverityBadge>` — shown as **Priority**, on the opportunity page only. A plain word plus a count
  of small bars, with no pill, ring or fill: Critical 3 bars in danger with the word in bone, High 2
  neutral bars, Medium 1, Low none.
- `<Badge tone>` — neutral, accent, success, warning, danger, info. 20px tall, 11px, 4px radius,
  inset ring. Used for "Demo data", "Planned", "Not configured". Finding status tags (Confirmed,
  Resolved) are neutral, and the default Open state shows nothing.
- `<HealthDot>` — a word plus a small mark, never coloured text: At risk is a 6px danger dot with
  the word in `ink-2`, Watch a hollow `ink-3` ring, Healthy no mark (its slot is kept so labels
  align). Below-target margins are the figure in semibold ink with one small danger dot; counts
  above contract are semibold ink with a neutral up arrow.
- `<Confidence>` — neutral bar plus the percentage (see §13.2).
- Action status — a dot that differs in **shape**: open is a hollow ring, in progress is filled info,
  resolved is filled success, dismissed is faint.

### 14.5 Feedback and non-ideal states

| State | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Loading, page | `<Spinner label>` — a lime spinner and a line of text, not a skeleton grid. |
| Loading, action | `<Button loading>`; the label stays, the spinner joins it. |
| Loading, blocking | A centred raised panel over the blurred overlay, naming the work. |
| Empty | `<EmptyState title body action>`: left-aligned, no icon tile. Where there is something useful to show instead, show it (the empty Actions page lists the top three open findings with a "Create action" link each). Always says what would fill it. |
| Inline message | `<Callout tone title>` — a full hairline in the tone, never a thick side stripe. |
| Form error | `role="alert"`, caption size, danger, directly under the field; a one-line summary in the form footer. |
| Toast | Bottom centre on a phone, bottom right on desktop; `aria-live="polite"`; success takes a lime tick, errors a danger icon and 6s instead of 3.5s. |
| Focus | A 2px accent outline at a 2px offset, globally. Never removed. |
| Disabled | 45% opacity plus `cursor-not-allowed`, and the reason in nearby text. |
| Demo / local mode | A sunken one-line banner under the app chrome with a neutral flask icon; on a phone it shortens to "Demo data: a fictional MSP, nothing is saved." |

## 18. Accessibility floor

This is a floor, not an aspiration. Anything below it is a defect.

- **Body text** meets 4.5:1 on its own surface. `--brand-faint` (3.0:1) is for icons and disabled
  states only.
- **Colour is never the only signal.** Severity carries a label and bar count; health carries a word;
  action status carries a shape; trends carry an arrow and screen-reader text; evidence highlights
  carry an underline as well as a fill.
- **Focus is always visible:** a 2px accent outline at 2px offset, globally. Row links keep the ring
  inside the card so a rounded clip cannot cut it off.
- **Charts** carry `role="img"` and a readable `aria-label`.
- **Reduced motion** is honoured globally and the landing animation is skipped entirely.
- **Targets** are at least 32px tall; the dense icon buttons carry padding to reach it.
- **Semantics:** one h1 per page, headings in order, real `<table>` markup with scoped headers, real
  `<select>` elements, `aria-live` on toasts, `aria-busy` on loading buttons.
- **Layout** survives 390px with no horizontal scroll, and survives 200% zoom.

## 21. Known drift and open gaps

Honest notes for whoever picks this up. None of these is a secret; all are small.

- **`og:image` and `twitter:image` are root-relative** (`/og-image.png`). They must become absolute
  URLs as soon as there is a domain, or no scraper will resolve them.
- **Marketing type is not tokenised.** `landing/primitives.tsx` sets the hero and section titles with
  `clamp()` on the display tracking token.
- **`Figure` has no size between 28px and 72px**, so the client page's leakage figure uses 28px where
  something larger would be right.
- **`TrendChart` only takes a fixed height**, so two cards side by side line up only for a given
  number of rows.
- **`--radius-2xl`, `elevate-1` and `glow-accent` are declared but unused.** They are reserved, not
  forgotten; delete them if they are still unused in three months.
- **Component colour transitions use Tailwind's `duration-150`** rather than `--duration-fast`
  (120ms) / `--duration-base` (200ms). New work should use the tokens.
- **`Highlighted` is duplicated** in `landing/primitives.tsx` and `app/findings/evidence.tsx`,
  because the app version pulls in the engine.
- **Two repricing figures used to disagree.** The engine's underpriced advice quoted the profit
  shortfall as the price rise; it now quotes the price that restores the target margin at the
  period's average costs (ABC Ltd: about £1,897 a month, +£47), matching the client page and the
  landing example. The finding's value is unchanged.
