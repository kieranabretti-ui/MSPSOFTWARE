# Board meeting: Headroom

7 October 2026. Three members, each applying one published framework: the Offers lens, the Monopoly lens and the Product lens. Memos are in `founder/board/`.

**3 fund if, 0 fund, 0 pass. Average score 4.7 out of 10.**

|---|---|---|
| Offers | 5 | FUND IF |

## Risks more than one member raised

1. **No proof on real data (all three).** Every figure comes from a fictional MSP. There are no customers, design partners or owner conversations, and the headline counts £336 twice.
2. **Too much effort before the first result (Offers, Product).** The owner has to export and upload CSVs and PDFs from several systems, with no integrations, export guides or guided route. For the Product lens, those first five minutes are the product, and nobody has tested them.
3. **The monthly fee may outlast the value (Offers, Monopoly).** Once the first audit clears the backlog, nothing says why the owner keeps paying. The demo's recurring leakage is £356 a month against a £249 price.
4. **No way to reach buyers (Offers, Monopoly).** No channel has been chosen and the cost of winning a customer is unknown.
5. **Trust gaps for sensitive data (Offers, Product).** No privacy policy, terms, data processing agreement, MFA or multi-user workspace, and the live site runs in browser-only mode.

Raised by one member only:
- **Monopoly:** no moat. Uploads are easy to copy and the software MSPs already use could add this as a feature.
- **Product:** no focus. There are five detectors plus a roadmap of seven more areas, and no single detector stands out.

## Conditions (checklist for the rest of the pack)

- [ ] Run guided audits on at least 5 real UK MSPs. Record what was found, what the owner accepted as real and what was billed. Turn them into anonymised case studies. (all three; tested by `/founder-consumer`)
- [ ] Show that the recurring leakage clearly exceeds the price, with overlaps removed. (Monopoly, Offers; `/founder-cfo`)
- [ ] Take the £336 overlap out of the headline total. (Product, Monopoly)
- [ ] Add a guided or done-for-you audit route and export guides for the common UK PSAs. Measure how long it takes to reach the first finding. (Offers, Product)
- [ ] Say what the subscription keeps catching each month after the first audit. (Offers; `/founder-pricing`, `/founder-offer`)
- [ ] Offer a guarantee tied to findings the owner accepts. (Offers; `/founder-offer`)
- [ ] Add templates for the conversation where the owner asks the client to pay. (Offers)
- [ ] Pick one or two detectors to lead with and park the wider roadmap. (Product)
- [ ] Choose one repeatable channel and measure its early conversion and cost per customer. (Monopoly, Offers; `/founder-marketing`, `/founder-cfo`)
- [ ] Map the competitors and write down what will stop a copycat, and which segment Headroom owns first. (Monopoly; `/founder-competitors`)
- [ ] Size the market. (Offers; `/founder-competitors`)
- [ ] Publish a privacy policy, terms and DPA, add MFA and switch on the backend before asking for real data. (Offers, Product)

## The members disagree on

- **What the edge is.** The Offers lens sees the edge in the evidence behind every pound and the money-based promise. The Monopoly lens says that isn't a moat: any PSA could add it. Its best hope is pooled benchmarks from many audits, which only exist after many customers.
- **How wide to go.** The founder's plan grows into "commercial intelligence for MSPs". The Product lens wants it cut to one or two detectors now. The Monopoly lens supports a small starting market, but expects the wider category to come later.

## The strongest version the board can see

This differs from the pitch. Headroom as pitched is a self-serve monthly subscription with five detectors. The strongest version the board sees starts as a **guided, evidence-backed audit** for UK MSPs, led by one or two detectors (most likely out-of-scope work against the contract, plus agreement drift). The owner hands over exports, gets a list of findings they can take into a client review, with templates for that conversation, and pays or keeps a guarantee based on findings they accept. The subscription then earns its place by catching new leakage before each invoice run, not by repeating the first audit. Every real audit adds to a benchmark of what "normal" leakage looks like for a UK MSP, which is the one thing a copycat can't build quickly. Integrations and the wider commercial intelligence roadmap come after the audits prove real money exists.
