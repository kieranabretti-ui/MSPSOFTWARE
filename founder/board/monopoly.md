# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The small market is well defined.** UK MSPs with 5 to 50 staff that run clients on monthly agreements is a narrow, nameable segment, which is where this lens wants a business to start. "Revenue leakage" is a tight first wedge, and the roadmap (profitability, pricing, contract optimisation, change orders) gives a believable way to expand from it.
- **The product makes a specific claim that can be checked.** It finds five types of leakage, and every pound comes with evidence, a calculation and a confidence level. That is a more defensible position than a dashboard. If it works on real data, the reasoning that tells "out of scope" from "covered" is the one place proprietary know-how could build up.
- **There is a possible data advantage.** Every audit run adds to a picture of how MSPs scope, price and leak money: benchmarks for typical drift, margin and unbilled rates. No competitor would have that on day one. It is the nearest thing to a scale advantage this business could build.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Distribution is undecided and there is no evidence yet.** There are no customers, no design partners, no conversations with MSP owners and no audits on real data, and how buyers will find the product is undecided. The buyers are short on time and sceptical of vendors. A good product with no route to the first 100 customers is not a business. It is also unknown what each customer would cost to acquire (open question for /founder-cfo and /founder-consumer).
2. **There is no moat against copycats or incumbents.** Data arrives as CSV and PDF uploads, and there are no integrations, no network effect and no brand yet. In this lens's terms it looks like an incremental improvement, not a ten-times breakthrough. The software MSPs already use for tickets, time and billing (PSA and accounting tools) holds the same data and could add leakage reports as a feature. Who already sells this, or something near it, to the same buyer is an open question for /founder-competitors.
3. **The value per customer may be too thin to support a durable position.** In the demo, a 15-client MSP shows £356 a month of recurring leakage, against a planned £249 a month for the Growth tier (a fictional dataset, and the headline includes a disclosed £336 overlap). If real audits find a one-off pile of leakage and then little each month, customers will run the audit once and cancel, and nothing builds up. Whether real MSPs leak enough each month to justify an ongoing subscription is unknown until real audits are run (open question for /founder-consumer and /founder-cfo).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the secret? What do you know about how MSPs leak revenue that the PSA vendors and MSP consultants do not?
- Who else is within reach of this buyer today, including features inside the tools they already pay for? (/founder-competitors)
- How do the first 100 MSPs arrive, through which channel, and what does each one cost? (/founder-consumer, /founder-cfo)
- Why now? What has changed that makes this possible or needed in 2026 and not five years ago?
- What do you own in month six that a copycat with a CSV parser does not: integrations, benchmark data, a channel, a brand?
- On real data, what share of the leakage found recurs each month, and how much is a one-off clean-up?
- Which subsegment could you take most of first: a region, MSPs on one PSA platform, or a peer group or community?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Real-data proof.** Run audits on at least a handful of real UK MSPs' data and show recurring leakage per client that comfortably exceeds the subscription price, after removing overlaps.
2. **A named distribution channel with a cost.** Pick one repeatable channel (for example a partnership, a peer community or a single PSA ecosystem), show early conversion from it, and put a measured or estimated acquisition cost against it (/founder-consumer, /founder-cfo).
3. **A competitor map and a moat plan.** Do a competitor scan of everyone within reach of this buyer (/founder-competitors), plus a written plan for what makes the business durable: which integrations, which benchmark data, and which small segment it will own first before expanding.
