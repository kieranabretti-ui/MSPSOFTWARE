# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The dream outcome is about money, and it is concrete.** "Find the work your MSP is doing for free" promises pounds, and every finding comes with its evidence, its calculation and a recommended action. Owners who started as technicians and distrust vendors can check each pound for themselves, and that is the strongest thing this offer has for getting believed.
- **The buyers have money and a reason to spend it.** UK MSPs with roughly £1m to £5m of revenue, billing clients monthly, can afford £99 to £499 a month. If the engine finds anything close to the demo rate on real data (about 2.9% of billing), the tool pays for itself on paper. Example: if a business really billed about £1m a year, 2.9% would be around £29k a year against a £2,988-a-year Growth plan. That is an example of the arithmetic only, not a claim about real results.
- **Time to first result can be short.** The free audit gives a finding on the first upload, so there is no long setup or rollout before the buyer sees value.
- **It leaves price competition alone.** It is positioned as a commercial result, not as a cheaper dashboard or reporting tool, which is the right side of the commodity line.
- **The founder already understands that proof has to be earned.** Calling findings "potential leakage to review" and confidence levels with reasons build trust. They are better than inflated claims.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No proof, so buyers cannot believe it will work for them.** The tool has not been run on any real MSP's data. It has no customers, design partners or testimonials, and the only figures come from a fictional MSP. Its £4,281 headline includes £336 that it counts twice. A sceptical buyer has no reason yet to believe the engine finds real money in messy real exports, or that the money it finds can actually be billed. Weak proof is the most common way an offer fails, and right now this one has none.
2. **Too much effort, and the work lands on the wrong person.** The buyer has to find, export and upload CSVs and contract PDFs from their own systems. There are no integrations, no guides for exporting from their PSA, and no done-for-you or guided route. That is a lot to ask of a time-poor owner before they see any value. Turning a finding into money also means an awkward conversation with the client, and the offer does not yet help with that.
3. **The monthly fee may outlast the value.** An audit finds the backlog once. After that, why keep paying each month? If the recurring findings are small next to the first audit, buyers will cancel once the first audit is done. Nothing in the offer yet (stack, guarantee, ongoing monitoring promise) answers this. The route to customers is also undecided, so we cannot tell yet whether getting a customer costs more than they pay before cancelling.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- When you run it on a real MSP's own exports, how much does it find, how much does the owner agree is real, and how much do they actually bill? (Get this from design-partner audits. Nobody has this number yet.)
- How many UK MSPs fit the 5 to 50 staff band, and is the number growing or shrinking as the sector consolidates? Open question for **/founder-competitors**.
- Who else within reach of this buyer already promises to find leakage or improve profitability, for example PSA add-ons, consultants or peer groups, and at what price? Open question for **/founder-competitors**.
- What does an MSP owner already pay to tackle this problem, and how do they talk about it? Open question for **/founder-consumer**.
- How do the first 100 customers arrive, what does each one cost, and how long does a paying customer stay? Open question for **/founder-cfo** once there is a channel to measure.
- What guarantee could you offer? For example: "If the audit doesn't find at least X times your first year's fee in leakage you agree is real, you pay nothing."
- Could you charge double, or take a share of the money actually recovered, and still be the obvious choice? Real audit results would tell you.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Run real audits first.** Run free audits on at least 5 real MSPs' data, with you guiding the uploads by hand. For each one, record how much the tool found, how much the owner accepted as real and how much was actually billed. Publish the results (anonymised, with permission) as case studies.
2. **Take the effort off the buyer.** Add a done-for-you or guided audit route, write export guides for the most common PSAs, and include templates for the client conversation (change orders, agreement uplift letters). Write down the obstacles buyers hit on the way to recovering the money, then add the cheap fixes to the offer one by one.
3. **Add a guarantee and a reason to keep paying.** Choose a guarantee tied to verified findings. State in the offer what the subscription keeps catching each month after the first audit. Then test whether £249 holds or should go up.
4. **Get the trust basics in place before handling real client data.** That means a privacy policy, terms, a data processing agreement and MFA, because without them this buyer will not hand over billing and contract data.
5. **Size the market and the cost of reaching it before scaling.** Get the market size and a named route to customers from /founder-competitors and /founder-consumer, and a first estimate of what each customer costs to win and is worth from /founder-cfo.
